# The commercial numbers

Read this before quoting any figure to the client. Several numbers in circulation contradict each other, and the resolutions below are the agreed ones.

## The target

**40 000 commercial sales by 30 November 2026.** This is the signed-off figure, matching the Phase 2 Growth OS.

Two other figures exist in older documents and are superseded:
- 41 000 appears in earlier drafts of the commercialisation plan. Use 40 000.
- 15 000 for 2026, with an R10 blended cost-per-sale guardrail, appears in the design system readme. It predates the current plan.

## What is actually happening

This is the part that matters for giving honest advice.

Google Analytics, 1 January to 19 August 2026: **167 765 users**, 8 549 705 views, 15 792 726 events, **0 key events**, **R0 revenue**.

Payments actually cleared in that period: **137**.

That is an overall conversion rate of roughly **0.08%**. The plan assumes **8%**, a hundredfold difference. The measured funnel from the same period:

| Step | Measured |
|---|---|
| /learn → packages viewed | 34.9% |
| packages → payment page | 67.2% |
| payment page → cleared | 1.72% (plan assumes 71.7%) |

The leak is overwhelmingly at the final step.

## What this means for content advice

At measured rates, the briefed R2.2m media budget buys roughly 335 sales, not 40 000. Reaching 40 000 at these rates would need approximately 9.9 million clicks.

So: **more traffic is not the answer until checkout conversion moves.** When a client asks for more content or more spend, the honest response is that both multiply a leak. The highest-value work right now is conversion tracking and the checkout itself.

Two caveats worth stating alongside those numbers, because they soften the picture slightly:

- Reaching the /payment page is not the same as attempting a checkout, so the 1.72% understates true checkout completion by an unknown margin.
- The existing app audience skews heavily to the free tier, which is not the audience the paid campaign is buying.

Neither caveat changes the conclusion. State them so the client knows the analysis is fair rather than alarmist.
