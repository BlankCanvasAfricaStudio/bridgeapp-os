# Audiences, by channel

Three channels, three genuinely different readers. The most common failure on this account is writing student copy for the Facebook parent audience, or writing parent copy for Instagram. The voice differences below are not stylistic preferences. They reflect who is holding the phone and what decision they are able to make.

---

## Facebook

**Who** Parents and Grade 12 families.

**Temperature** Warm. Largely July leads being retargeted, plus organic page followers. They already know who BridgeApp is; they have not yet booked.

**What they are actually feeling** That they are behind, that the process has more moving parts than they can hold, and that a mistake now costs their child a year. They are not short of motivation. They are short of a system.

**Voice** Reassuring and structured. The anchoring line for the whole channel is *"You don't have to figure this out alone."* Declarative sentences. Address the parent directly in the second person. Never talk about their child in the third person as a "learner" or a "candidate". Say "your child".

**What they can do** They hold the credit card and they book the consultation. This is the conversion channel.

**CTA** `Sign up now › /signup`

**Action wanted** Book the free 30-minute application and subject review.

**Do not** Use student slang, emoji-led openings, or anything that reads as though it was written for a teenager. Do not use hashtags.

---

## Instagram

**Who** Students and gap-year students, Grades 10 to 12.

**Temperature** Warm and engaged. Heavily weighted toward people who have finished the Pathfinder quiz and seen a result.

**What they are actually feeling** Uncertainty about what they are allowed to want, and a quiet fear that they have already made a decision that closed a door. Gap-year students carry a specific version of this: that re-deciding means falling behind.

**Voice** Direct and motivational. The anchoring line is *"Know your next move."* Short sentences. Address the student in the second person. Confidence without hype: this audience detects and rejects marketing tone faster than the parents do.

**What they can do** They cannot usually pay. They can finish the quiz, save a post, send it to a parent, and answer a story poll. Treat Instagram as the channel that creates demand which Facebook and email then convert.

**CTA** `Sign up today › link in bio`

**Action wanted** Finish the Pathfinder quiz, save the post, and get a parent to book.

**What performs** Save-bait. Checklists, "screenshot this", mistakes-to-avoid, APS explainers. Result cards from the quiz. Real acceptance letters. Creator collabs where a student speaks in their own words.

**Do not** Lead with a title-card slide on a carousel. Do not use more than four hashtags.

---

## LinkedIn

**Who** The professional side of the audience: parents at their desks, educators, school leaders, and partner organisations.

**Temperature** Cold to warm. Much of this is reach into people who have not met the brand.

**What they are actually feeling** Professionally interested in the systemic problem, not personally panicked. A school leader reading this is thinking about their whole Matric cohort.

**Voice** Direct, but carrying more proof and perspective than the consumer channels. State the systemic problem, then the product as the response. This is the channel where "Most Matrics don't miss university on marks. They miss it on admin." lands hardest, because it reframes something the reader already half-knew.

**What they can do** Sign up personally, or open a school partnership conversation. LinkedIn is the channel that feeds Growth Engine 2, the school outreach programme.

**CTA** `Sign up now: app.bridgeapp.co.za/learn`

**Action wanted** Individual sign-up, or a school-level conversation.

**Format** Creative at 1200×1200. Multi-page documents export as carousel PDFs.

**Hashtags** Three to four, professional register: `#UniversityApplications` `#MatricClassOf2026` `#UniApply` `#SouthAfricanEducation` `#AdmissionRequirements` `#MatricParents`

---

## The bridge between channels

The channels are designed to hand off, and content should be written knowing where it goes next:

- Instagram creates a student who wants something → the student pushes a parent to Facebook or the site
- Facebook converts the parent into a booked consultation
- LinkedIn opens the school, which delivers a cluster of both

A post that works on all three usually works properly on none. Rewrite per channel rather than cross-posting.
