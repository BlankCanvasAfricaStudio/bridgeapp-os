# Boosting and paid amplification

Boosting is where content spend quietly disappears, because the decision usually gets made on taste. These five rules exist to make it a numbers decision instead.

## The five rules

**1. Boost the week's best organic performer, and only that.**
Chosen every Monday from the previous week's actual numbers. Retargeting budget only. The logic is simple: organic reach has already told you which post the audience wants. Paying to distribute a post the audience ignored organically is paying to be ignored faster.

**2. Two conversion posts per channel per week, minimum.**
Every one carries a sign-up CTA. This is the floor, not the target. Value content earns attention and reports beautifully; it does not produce leads. A month that misses this floor will show good reach and no consultations.

**3. One CTA per post. Never two.**
A post that asks for two things gets neither. Applies to organic and paid equally.

**4. Carousels lead with a save-worthy first slide.**
Saves are the ranking signal that carries the rest of the slides. Instagram first, but it holds on Facebook too.

**5. Creator collab, Tier-1 student, in their own words.**
Own results, single tracked link, roughly monthly on Instagram. The tracked link matters, an untracked collab cannot be judged and will be renewed on vibes.

## Choosing what to boost

Rank last week's posts by **saves and shares first, then link clicks**, not by likes or reach. Saves and shares indicate the content did a job for the person; likes indicate they scrolled past approvingly.

If two posts are close, boost the one with the clearer single CTA.

If nothing cleared the bar, meaning no post materially outperformed the others, do not boost that week. Roll the budget forward. An underwhelming week is information, and spending through it destroys the signal.

## Pricing a boost

Work backwards from the sale, not forwards from the budget.

```
consultations needed  =  sales wanted ÷ 0.20        (20% consult-to-sale)
leads needed          =  consultations ÷ 0.20       (20% lead-to-consult)
reach needed          =  leads ÷ 0.006              (0.6% reach-to-lead)
```

Those rates are the engine's targets, not measured performance. Once real numbers exist, substitute them. The model is only as good as the rates in it.

The campaign's CPA gate is **R30 a sale** during the validation sprint. That is a gate the sprint has to prove, not a planning assumption. For planning beyond the sprint, the benchmark band for a South African B2C subscription at this price point runs R55 optimised to R85 conservative.

The briefed media split is **85% Meta, 15% LinkedIn**.

## The honest caveat

At the time of writing, the Google Analytics property records **zero key events and R0 revenue**. No purchase event fires with a value attached. That means source-level CPA cannot currently be measured, only modelled.

Say this plainly whenever CPA comes up in a client conversation, rather than quoting a modelled number as though it were measured. The first fix in any paid-media conversation is conversion tracking, not creative.
