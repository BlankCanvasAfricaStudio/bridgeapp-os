The ask

R13 000 of new spend, approved by Friday 21 August, so week one opens on Monday morning.

All of it goes into Meta, to buy roughly 3 946 measured clicks. The R2 000 already unlocked covers fuel for the 29 school visits, and printing is done on our own machines at no cost. The budget is set deliberately above what a bare test needs, so that money can be moved between audiences and creative mid week rather than waiting on the next approval. Nothing here is a launch. It is a measurement, run with enough room to act on what it shows.

R13 000 · Meta R2 000 · Fuel, unlocked R0 · Print, in house
What this document is not asking for. No commitment to the full campaign budget. That decision belongs at the end of the fourteen day sprint, on evidence, and the gate for it is already written into the sprint workbook.
Why this week

We know what a click costs. We do not know what a sale costs.

The Meta boost that ran from 29 July spent R1 617.57 and returned 491 link clicks. That is R3.29 a click, real and measured. It is the only true cost figure the campaign holds. Everything after the click is dark: the lead endpoint is not live, so nobody knows how many clicks become sign-ups, and GA4 carries no purchase event, so no click has ever been tied to a payment.

The two engines

What each engine is doing, and what it is for

Both sell direct to the family. Both are measured on the same basis, cost per sale, so that at the end of the sprint they can be compared and the budget can follow whichever one works. Nothing in this sprint chases a school contract.

Growth Engine 1

Commercial Sales

Buying attention from parents who can pay, and finding out what that attention converts at.

Audience
The paying parent, 35 to 44, mostly women. This is not a guess. The July buy reported 60.7% women against 38.4% men, and 35 to 44 was the largest age band. Geographically it landed Gauteng first, then KwaZulu-Natal at 1 200, Western Cape 863, Limpopo 572, Eastern Cape 556, Mpumalanga 556. We are buying the same audience again because it is the one that holds the card.
Channels
  • Meta, paid. All R13 000. The conversion channel and the only one with real cost data.
  • Facebook, organic. Five posts. Parent facing, reassuring, structured.
  • Instagram, organic. Five posts. Students, who create the demand a parent then acts on.
  • LinkedIn, organic. Three posts. School leadership, which feeds Engine 2 rather than selling directly.
Deviation, week one only The briefed media split is 85% Meta and 15% LinkedIn. We are putting 100% behind Meta this week. Splitting a small test budget across a platform with zero performance history halves the signal on both. LinkedIn paid enters in week two, once Meta's click to sale rate is known.
Content
Four paid creative variants, run against the same audience with distinct tracking, so the test reads cleanly:
  • A 20 second recut of the July reel. The original runs 53 seconds against an average watch time of 7 seconds, so we are currently paying to deliver 46 seconds nobody sees. Hook rate is healthy at 38%; hold rate is 7.6%. The fix is length, not concept.
  • A static statement graphic. No video cost, tests whether the hook or the format is doing the work.
  • A product walkthrough screen recording. Answers the objection "what am I actually paying for".
  • A parent proof card. Borrowed trust, one CTA.
Thirteen organic posts across the fixed weekly slots, minimum two carrying a conversion call to action per channel. Monday opens on the 37 day countdown, Wednesday carries the offer, Thursday the proof, Friday the funding checklist, Sunday the question harvest. All produced inside the retainer at no additional cost.
Budget
R13 000 media, new. Creative production carried in the retainer. Printing done in house at no cost.
Expected output
3 946 measured clicks, delivered inside R13 000. That figure we can commit to, because it is budget divided by a known cost per click. The number of sales those clicks produce is the thing being measured and we will not pretend to forecast it. On the range above it falls between 20 and 158.
Growth Engine 2

School Outreach

Five days, 29 schools, Pretoria and Centurion. Getting in front of learners and their parents where they already are, and selling the same product on the same terms as paid media.

Audience
Grade 11 and 12 learners, and the parents behind them. The staff contact at each school is how we get through the door, not who we are selling to. Three of the schools are contacts we have spoken to before and are re opening: Iona Convent, Loreto Convent and Pretoria High for Girls.
The route
  • Mon, Pretoria West. Pepps Motheong, Acudeo Kirkney, Elandspoort, Pretoria-Wes, Pretoria Technical, Royal Schools Queens. Six schools, close to the start point to maximise same day reach.
  • Tue, Pretoria East. CBC Mount Edmund, Crawford Pretoria, Curro Hazeldean, Woodhill, Maragon Mooikloof, Tyger Valley. Six schools, new private school relationships.
  • Wed, Pretoria Central. Iona Convent, Loreto Convent, Pretoria High for Girls, Pretoria Boys High, The Glen High, Abbots College. Six schools, re engaging the stalled ones.
  • Thu, Pretoria East and Central. St Alban’s, Clapham High, St Mary’s DSG, SPARK Silver Lakes, Southdowns. Five independent schools with strong learner populations.
  • Fri, Centurion. SPARK Centurion, Lyttleton Manor, Curro Thatchfield, Sutherland, Amberfield, PLG Raslouw. Six schools, closing the week on opportunities raised earlier.
Channels
  • In person, learner facing. 29 visits across five days. Where we get in front of learners on the day we capture sign ups there and then. Where we do not, we book the engagement and log it.
  • Tagged QR at the point of contact. One code per school. A learner scans it, or takes the slip home to a parent who does. This is how a school visit becomes a traceable sale.
  • Take home slip for the parent. The learner is the carrier. The parent is the buyer.
  • LinkedIn, three posts aimed at parents at their desks, naming Pretoria and Centurion the same week the team is in those corridors. A parent seeing their own child’s school named is a materially warmer lead.
Content
  • 29 tagged QR codes, one per school, registered in the UTM register before the first visit. A school visited without one produces sales nobody can trace back to it.
  • Learner facing leave behind, carrying that school’s code. Printed in house.
  • Take home parent slip. Same code, written for the person who pays.
  • Paper sign up sheet as the fallback where signal or phones fail, captured the same day.
Budget
R2 000 fuel, already unlocked. Nothing further requested. Roughly 855 km across the week, estimated at R1 749, with R250 for traffic, route changes and follow up visits. Leave behinds and parent slips are printed in house. Coordinator time carried in the retainer.
Expected output
  • 29 visits completed, every one logged the same day with learners reached and sign ups captured.
  • A first measured sign up rate per visit. Nobody has ever counted this, so the week sets the baseline rather than hitting a target.
  • Cost per sale for the school engine, on exactly the same basis as paid media, so the two can be put side by side on Sunday and the money can follow whichever works.
School partnerships are deliberately out of scope this sprint. Both engines sell direct to the family, and both are scored on the same thing: what a sale costs. Contracts and formal partnerships with these schools are not chased here and are not measured here. They arrive later and they arrive on their own evidence, once a school can see we are already supporting their learners well. That proof is a by product of doing the B2C job properly, which is why the B2C job is the only one in this sprint.

The sprint closes against a seven test gate already built into the workbook: qualifying sales banked, blended cost per sale at or under target, each engine inside twice target, at least one engine at or under target, spend inside the cap, and a complete log. That gate decides whether the full budget is released, released into one engine only, held, or the target is re-cut. Exco makes that call, on that evidence, not before.
