04

Standard procedures

The seven jobs that repeat. Written so a new person can do them correctly on their first day without asking, which is the only real test of an SOP.

Publishing a postContent Producer
Every posting slot, 13 a week
  1. Find the slot in the content calendar. The day decides the job: Wednesday is the offer, Thursday is proof, Friday is a checklist or funding, Sunday is Q&A, Monday is the deadline countdown.
  2. Check the deadline if the post carries one. Recount the days from the publication date. Never copy a day count forward from an earlier post.
  3. Write the caption finished. No placeholders, no bracketed choices. One call to action, never two.
  4. Build the link in the UTM sheet and copy it from the finished-link column. Never type a link by hand.
  5. Check the channel voice. Parent voice on Facebook, student voice on Instagram, professional voice on LinkedIn. A post that reads the same on all three is wrong on at least two.
  6. Schedule it in the slot, and mark the calendar row as scheduled.
Making the Monday boost decisionPerformance Marketer
Every Monday, before the 08:30
  1. Pull last week's organic numbers for both consumer channels.
  2. Rank by saves and shares first, then link clicks. Not by likes or reach. Saves mean the post did a job for someone; likes mean they scrolled past approvingly.
  3. Pick one post. If two are close, take the one with the clearer single call to action.
  4. If nothing clearly won, boost nothing and roll the budget forward. An underwhelming week is information and spending through it destroys the signal.
  5. Retargeting audiences only. Boosting into cold audiences is a different exercise and needs its own budget line.
  6. Log the decision and the reason in the working book, so next Monday can see whether it worked.
Running a school visitSchools Coordinator
Per school, during cluster weeks
  1. Confirm the week before, with a named contact at the school and a written time.
  2. Take a QR code tagged to that school, built from the UTM sheet with the school in the content field. Untagged codes make the visit unmeasurable.
  3. Brief the hall on the readiness tracker. It is what a learner can do on their phone before you have left the car park, and it is the route we can measure.
  4. Capture the contact for the school, not just the learners: the Life Orientation teacher or head of department is who lets you back in.
  5. Log the visit the same day in the working book, with QR scans if the numbers are available.
  6. Tick the school in the tracker's schools list so the cluster's progress stays visible to everyone.
Writing the weekly reportCampaign Lead
Every Friday, posted by 15:00
  1. Pull the week's actuals into the working book: reach, leads, sales, spend.
  2. Pro-rate the monthly targets by the fraction of the month elapsed. Not by quarters, because months are not four weeks.
  3. Diagnose by which stage is short, never by the headline. Reach short is distribution, so look at spend and posting frequency. Reach fine but leads short means the content entertains and does not convert, which is the most common failure, so check the two-conversion-posts-a-week floor. Leads fine but sales short is downstream of us, so bring the pattern to Gradesmatch on Wednesday rather than rewriting content that is working.
  4. Name what to boost Monday, with the number that chose it.
  5. List only decisions the client must make. If there are none, say none.
  6. Label every figure measured or modelled. Until the purchase event is live, anything downstream of a click is modelled and should say so.
Handling a leadCampaign Lead, then Advisor
Within 24 hours, every lead
  1. Leads arrive from the readiness tracker with a name, email address, WhatsApp number and their readiness score.
  2. Send the breakdown within 24 hours, on WhatsApp and email. The page promises this, so it is a commitment and not a nicety.
  3. Route by grade. A Grade 9 to 11 learner goes to PathFinder, not UniApply, because selling the wrong product loses a two-year relationship. Matric and gap-year go to UniApply.
  4. Hand every UniApply lead to Gradesmatch the same day. What happens next is theirs. Ours is that it arrives fast, complete and correctly tagged so the sale can be traced back to the campaign that produced it.
  5. Follow up once. The page says once, so follow up once.
  6. POPIA applies. Consent was given by the parent or guardian for this purpose. Do not repurpose the list.
Adding a campaign linkAnyone who makes a link
Before any link goes anywhere public
  1. Open the UTM link builder and add a row. Date, owner, channel.
  2. Pick source and medium from the dropdowns. If the value you need is not there, ask rather than invent. One improvised medium creates a channel that never rejoins the others.
  3. Name the campaign and the creative: season, product, objective for the campaign; format and variant for the content. The variant suffix is what lets you tell two creatives apart later.
  4. Copy the finished link from column I. Never retype it.
  5. Never hand-tag Google Ads. Manual tags override auto-tagging and lose the cost data. Link the accounts instead.
Escalating a blockerAnyone
The moment something is stuck
  1. Say it in the channel the same day. Not in the next meeting. A blocker held quietly for three days is three days lost.
  2. State what is blocked, by what, and what it costs. "Waiting on the lead endpoint, so every lead since Tuesday is unreachable" is actionable. "Still waiting on dev" is not.
  3. Anything blocked over a week goes on the Wednesday agenda automatically, with the cost attached.
  4. Anything blocking the target goes to the client the same week, not the same month.

The tracker saves per device, not per teamAnything typed into the commercialisation tracker, the funnel inputs, the spend figures, the ticked schools, is stored in that person's browser only. Two people will see two different sets of numbers and neither will know. So the tracker is the model and the reference, and the working book in Google Sheets is the record. Numbers the team acts on live in the sheet.
Nothing pulls itself in yetThere is no automatic feed from Meta, Instagram, LinkedIn or GA4 into any of these documents. Numbers arrive as screenshots or CSV exports and someone types them into the working book on a Friday. That is fine at this size and it is what the Friday slot is for. If you want it automated later, a connector such as Supermetrics or Windsor.ai bridges GA4 and the ad platforms, and that is a paid tool decision rather than a build.
What has to come from the dev team before any of this is realThree things, all specified in the instrumentation brief: the GA4 purchase event fired from the Peach webhook, the lead endpoint the readiness tracker posts to, and the landing page deployed on the BridgeApp domain so it can be tagged. Until the first of those lands, every cost per sale in every document is a model, and the team should say so out loud whenever one is quoted.
